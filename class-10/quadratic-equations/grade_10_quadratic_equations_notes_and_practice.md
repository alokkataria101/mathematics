# Quadratic Equations --- Grade 10 (USA)

## 1. What is a quadratic equation?

A **quadratic equation** is an equation in which the highest power of
the variable is 2.

The standard form is:

\[ ax\^2 + bx + c = 0 \]

where: - (a), (b), and (c) are numbers (called **coefficients**). - (a
`\ne 0`{=tex}). If (a=0), the equation is not quadratic. - (x) is the
variable.

**Examples** - (x\^2 + 5x + 6 = 0) --- quadratic - (2x\^2 - 3x + 1 = 0)
--- quadratic - (4x + 7 = 0) --- not quadratic (the highest power is 1)

## 2. Understanding the coefficients

In (2x\^2 - 7x + 3 = 0): - (a=2) - (b=-7) - (c=3)

**Remember:** Include the sign with each coefficient. In (-x\^2+4x-5=0),
for example, (a=-1), (b=4), and (c=-5).

## 3. What does it mean to solve a quadratic equation?

To **solve** an equation means to find the value or values of (x) that
make the equation true.

For example:

\[ x\^2 - 5x + 6 = 0 \]

Factor the left side:

\[ (x-2)(x-3)=0 \]

If two numbers multiply to give zero, at least one of them must be zero.
Therefore:

\[ x-2=0 `\quad `{=tex}`\text{or}`{=tex} `\quad `{=tex}x-3=0 \]

So:

\[ `\boxed{x=2 \text{ or } x=3}`{=tex} \]

Both values are solutions.

## 4. Three common ways to solve quadratic equations

### Method A: Factoring

Factoring rewrites an expression as a product of simpler expressions.

Example:

\[ x\^2+7x+12=0 \]

Find two numbers that: - Multiply to (12). - Add to (7).

Those numbers are (3) and (4).

\[ (x+3)(x+4)=0 \]

Set each factor equal to zero:

\[ x+3=0 `\quad `{=tex}`\text{or}`{=tex} `\quad `{=tex}x+4=0 \]

\[ `\boxed{x=-3 \text{ or } x=-4}`{=tex} \]

### Method B: Square-root method

Use this method when the equation can be written as (x\^2=k).

Example:

\[ x\^2=25 \]

Take the square root of both sides:

\[ x=`\pm`{=tex}`\sqrt{25}`{=tex} \]

\[ `\boxed{x=5 \text{ or } x=-5}`{=tex} \]

The symbol (`\pm`{=tex}) means **plus or minus**. Remember to include
both roots when taking the square root of a positive number.

### Method C: Quadratic formula

The quadratic formula can solve any quadratic equation written in
standard form:

\[ ax\^2+bx+c=0 \]

The formula is:

\[ `\boxed{x=\frac{-b\pm\sqrt{b^2-4ac}}{2a}}`{=tex} \]

Example:

\[ x\^2-4x+3=0 \]

Here, (a=1), (b=-4), and (c=3).

Substitute into the formula:

\[ x=`\frac{-(-4)\pm\sqrt{(-4)^2-4(1)(3)}}{2(1)}`{=tex} \]

\[ x=`\frac{4\pm\sqrt{16-12}}{2}`{=tex} =`\frac{4\pm2}{2}`{=tex} \]

Therefore:

\[ `\boxed{x=3 \text{ or } x=1}`{=tex} \]

## 5. The discriminant: How many real solutions?

The expression under the square root in the quadratic formula is called
the **discriminant**:

\[ D=b\^2-4ac \]

It tells us how many real solutions the equation has:

  Discriminant   Number of real solutions
  -------------- ------------------------------
  (D\>0)         Two different real solutions
  (D=0)          One repeated real solution
  (D\<0)         No real solutions

For now, focus on recognizing the three cases. You can calculate the
discriminant by identifying (a), (b), and (c), then evaluating
(b\^2-4ac).

## 6. Connection to graphs

The graph of a quadratic function,

\[ y=ax\^2+bx+c, \]

is a U-shaped or upside-down U-shaped curve called a **parabola**.

-   If (a\>0), the parabola opens upward.
-   If (a\<0), the parabola opens downward.
-   The solutions of (ax\^2+bx+c=0) are the **x-intercepts** of the
    graph, where the curve crosses or touches the x-axis.

A parabola may cross the x-axis twice, touch it once, or not meet it at
all. These correspond to two, one, or zero real solutions.

## 7. Quick reminders

-   Write the equation in standard form before identifying (a), (b), and
    (c).
-   Standard form means one side is zero: (ax\^2+bx+c=0).
-   Keep negative signs when identifying coefficients.
-   When using the zero-product property, set each factor equal to zero.
-   Check a solution by substituting it into the original equation.

**11.** The graph of a quadratic function crosses the x-axis at (x=-2)

Let the width be (x) feet.

a.  Write an equation for the area.

b.  Put the equation in standard form.

c.  Solve the equation and state the garden's width and length. (Use the
    positive dimensions.)
